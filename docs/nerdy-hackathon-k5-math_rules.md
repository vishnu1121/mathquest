## 1. What the challenge is

A free, public AI build challenge run by Nerdy LLC (NYSE: NRDY), the Live + AI learning platform behind Varsity Tutors. You build and demo an AI-powered learning tool. Cost to enter is $0.

The site frames the real prize as visibility: strong submissions can open the door to AI Product Engineer roles at Nerdy, hiring in the US, Latin America and India. Finalists present live at Demo Day to Nerdy executive and engineering leadership.

Company stats listed on the page: 3,000+ subjects taught, 40k+ experts matched by AI on 100+ attributes, 4.9/5 average session rating, 40+ live AI "superpowers" shipped to learners.

## 2. The prompts

Three suggested prompts plus an open option. The prompts are described as examples, not requirements.

### Prompt 01 — Math: K–5 Math Game

Build an interactive, gamified math experience aimed at elementary students that makes foundational arithmetic concepts intuitive and engaging.

What Nerdy says it is looking for: innovative mechanics that encourage steady progression and reward mastery of core numeracy skills.

That is the entire text of the prompt on the site. There is no further spec, no rubric, no required feature list, and no sample data published for it.

### Prompt 02 — Language: Language Learning App

A structured, user-friendly mobile experience that simplifies learning a new language through spaced repetition or immersive daily practice. Looking for: strong pedagogical design with a seamless, intuitive user journey.

### Prompt 03 — Literacy: English Reading Game

A literacy application helping young learners improve reading fluency, comprehension and vocabulary through interactive storytelling or challenge-based gameplay. Looking for: educational rigor blended with fun, narrative-driven design.

### Open — Bring your own idea

Anything that genuinely helps someone learn. Same bar as above: a real learner, a real problem, something demoable.

## 4. How to enter (Rules §4)

Complete and submit the form at hackathon.nerdy.com during the entry period.

### Required elements

1. **A working product or experience** whose purpose is to help a person learn.
2. **A written description** covering what it does, how you built it, and what you would do next to make it operational.
3. **A demo video, no longer than three minutes,** showing the entry functioning. Judges are not required to watch past three minutes. Provide it as a link on a generally accessible platform (YouTube, Loom, Drive or Vimeo, unlisted is fine) or upload the file through the form.
4. **The disclosures required by Rules 7.6** (third-party materials, open-source components and their licenses, third-party APIs, datasets, models, fonts, images, audio, plus the nature and extent of any generative AI assistance used).

Note: the timeline section of the landing page describes the video as 2–3 minutes; the rules state a three-minute maximum.

### Optional but recommended

- Link to source code repo
- Link to a live deployment
- Supporting files

The site says these are likely to help judges evaluate further but are not required.

### Form fields on the page

Company (optional), Your name (required), Email (required), What did you build? (required), Which prompt? (K–5 Math Game / Language Learning App / English Reading Game / My own idea), Demo video (required, link or upload), Code repo, Live demo, Anything else, plus agreement to the contest terms.

File uploads only become enabled once name, email and the description are filled in.

### Other entry rules

- **New or substantially updated work (4.4).** The entry must be created during the entry period, or if it existed beforehand, must have been substantially developed during the entry period. You must describe what was developed during the entry period.
- **Language (4.5).** All materials in English, or with an English translation of the video, description and any instructions.
- **Access for evaluation (4.6).** Keep the entry available to Nerdy free of charge and without restriction, for evaluation and testing, until the end of the judging period.
- **AI assistance (4.7).** Building with generative AI assistance is expressly permitted and encouraged, subject to the 7.6 disclosure. What you submit must be work you directed and can explain.
- **One entry per person (4.9).** If you submit more than once, the most recent entry received before close counts and the rest are disregarded.
- **Changes (§5).** You can revise any time before the period closes. After close, no changes, except a narrow modification Nerdy may permit to remove infringing material, exposed personal info or inappropriate content, and the entry must stay substantively the same.

## 5. Hard constraints on what you build

These come from Rules 4.8, 7.6 and 7.7. Treat them as a build checklist.

- **No copyleft or reciprocal licenses.** No GPL, LGPL, AGPL or SSPL components (7.6).
- **No malicious code.** No virus, worm, spyware, backdoor or disabling code (7.7e).
- **No biometrics.** The entry must not collect or contain biometric identifiers or biometric information, must not perform facial recognition or voiceprint identification, and must not infer emotion, attention or affective state from face, voice or other biometric data. Speech recognition, transcription and pronunciation assessment that do not identify a speaker **are** permitted (7.7f).
- **No identifiable people other than you.** No personal information of any third party (7.7g).
- **No real student data.** Not developed or tested using real student data, and not tested with data of anyone under 13 without verifiable parental consent (7.7h).
- **No confidential or trade-secret material of any third party** (7.7d).
- **Original and unencumbered.** You are sole author and owner, or hold written assignment from every other contributor. No infringement of copyright, patent, trademark, trade secret, moral right, privacy or publicity right (7.7a, 7.7b).
- **No conflicting obligations.** Your participation must not breach any employment, invention-assignment, confidentiality or consulting agreement (7.7c, 3.4).
- **No prohibited content.** Nothing unlawful, defamatory, harassing, obscene, sexually explicit, or inappropriate in an educational context (4.8).
- **No preferential support from Nerdy.** The entry must not be derived from work developed with funding, investment, contract, commercial license or other preferential support from Nerdy before the close of the entry period (3.5).

Relevance for a K–5 build: the under-13 data rule and the biometric rule both bite directly. Use synthetic or self-generated data only, and avoid webcam or mic-based attention/engagement detection. Speech recognition for answers is allowed as long as it does not identify the speaker.

## 6. Judging (Rules §6)

- **Judges.** A panel selected by Nerdy. May be employees, officers, contractors or third parties. May or may not be named on the site. Can be added, removed or replaced before or during the judging period.
- **Human evaluation (6.2).** All evaluation and prize determination is done by humans. No automated or AI-assisted scoring or ranking. No analysis of any entrant's face, voice or biometric characteristics.
- **Threshold review (6.3).** Each entry is first checked for whether it satisfies §4 and is not disqualified. Entries that fail do not advance.
- **Evaluation (6.4).** Judged at the judges' sole and absolute discretion. Nerdy is not obligated to apply any particular criteria or methodology and may consider any factor it considers relevant. Internal evaluation guidance may exist, may change at any time, and Nerdy is not obligated to publish it.
- **No obligation to test (6.5).** Judges are not required to install, execute or test any entry. They may evaluate on the description, video, repository and accompanying materials alone.
- **Finality (6.6).** Judges' decisions are final and binding, including interpretation of the rules.
- **No award required (6.7).** Nerdy may award fewer prizes, or none, if no entry is of sufficient merit.
- **Separate from hiring (6.8).** Scoring and prize selection do not determine any hiring decision.

Practical read: since judges may never run your code, the three-minute video and the written description carry most of the weight.

## 8. Eligibility (Rules §3)

**Who may enter:** natural persons who, as of the start of the entry period, are at least 18 and at least the age of majority where they live, and are legal residents of the United States (50 states and DC, excluding American Samoa, Guam, Northern Mariana Islands, Puerto Rico, US Virgin Islands), Argentina, Colombia, Costa Rica, or India.

**Individuals only (3.2).** Prizes go to one natural person. If you collaborated with others, you must comply with 7.7(a), which means holding a written assignment of all rights from every other contributor.

**Who may not enter (3.3):** Nerdy employees, officers, directors, members, managers, agents or representatives, and those of any parent, subsidiary or affiliate; anyone under an active consulting services contract with Nerdy other than delivering tutoring or instruction on the platform; anyone involved in designing, administering, promoting, judging or fulfilling the challenge, and any judge; immediate family or household members of the above; residents of OFAC-sanctioned jurisdictions or anywhere the challenge or prize award is restricted; anyone whose participation would create an actual or apparent conflict of interest.

Learners, customers and platform users are eligible. Tutors, instructors and experts who deliver instruction through the Nerdy platform or marketplace are eligible, unless otherwise excluded above.

You may keep developing it and present it as a portfolio work without notice to or consent from Nerdy. Survives termination and any disqualification.
- **7.3 Fallback license.** If the assignment is held unenforceable anywhere, Nerdy instead gets an exclusive (or non-exclusive if exclusive is not permitted) perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, transferable and sublicensable license to exploit the entry for any purpose, including incorporating it into Nerdy products.

- **7.9 No obligation.** Nothing obligates Nerdy to review, evaluate, use, develop, publish, commercialize, return or preserve any entry, or to pay anything beyond a prize actually awarded.

Nerdy states it is independently developing products addressing the same learning problems and many may be similar or identical to your entry in concept, function or design. No compensation is owed for that.
- **7.11 Remedies.** Limited to an action at law for damages. No injunctive or equitable relief.

**7.8 Indemnity.** You defend and indemnify Nerdy and its affiliates against any claim arising from a breach or alleged breach of 7.6 or 7.7, or from Nerdy's use of the entry in reliance on them.

## 12. Your information (Rules §12)

**Collected:** name, email, description of your entry, prompt selected, demo video link or file, code repo and live deployment links if provided, the 7.6 disclosures, any additional files. Recorded automatically: referring page and marketing link, browser user-agent string, and a salted hash of your IP address (stored as a hash, used only to rate-limit submissions and uploads).

**Used for:** administering and judging, contacting you about your entry, verifying eligibility and paying prizes, considering you for current and future roles, measuring which channels entries came from.

**Seen by:** Nerdy personnel involved in administering, judging and recruiting, plus infrastructure service providers under contract. Entries are held on a restricted internal page and are not loaded into Nerdy's ATS or CRM. Information is not sold.

**Retention:** six months from close of the entry period, unless you ask for earlier deletion, you consented to longer so they can contact you about future roles, or law requires longer.

**Your choices:** request access, correction or deletion, or withdraw your entry, by emailing hackathon@nerdy.com.
